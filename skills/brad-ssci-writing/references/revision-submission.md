# Revision and submission packages

## Author-led changes

Use the latest explicitly identified files. Compare versions before assuming a copy, rename, or save operation changed the research. Treat author edits and comments as the primary revision brief. Do not revert accepted wording because an older draft or generic style guide preferred something else.

Classify changes into requested local wording, necessary dependent synchronization, and substantive issues requiring discussion. Only perform mutations within the current authorization. A request to check files is read-only unless the author also asks for corrections.

Do not reply inside Word comments when asked simply to make changes. Do not silently resolve every comment or accept every tracked change if preserving a review trail matters. Keep unrelated edits intact.

Substantive concerns include changed numerical results, uncertain source support, parameter selection, scope, author order, correspondence status, and CRediT contributions. Explain the concrete issue to the author before changing it; do not manufacture a new interpretation to smooth the prose.

## Word edits and typography

Use an appropriate document workflow that preserves native equations, hyperlinks, managed citation fields, figures, and tables. Inspect source and rendered pages as relevant; plain-text extraction cannot establish that a layout is sound.

For Brad's English Word manuscripts, use these as defaults only when the author or journal has not chosen another format:

- Times New Roman, with consistent font size for the same paragraph role.
- Body text around 12 pt; table text 10.5 pt (Chinese 五号), including intentional exceptions when legibility or journal rules require them.
- Supplementary body paragraphs flush left, without first-line indentation, and separated by paragraph spacing rather than repeated empty paragraphs.
- English typographic apostrophes and quotation marks in the same Latin font as surrounding text. Preserve English curly apostrophes when chosen; do not replace every apostrophe with an ASCII mark in the name of consistency.
- Check for stray spaces around possessives, broken symbols, mixed fallback fonts, inconsistent bolding, and the same type of paragraph changing size mid-document.

These are author preferences, not universal SSCI requirements. Do not apply them blindly to mathematics, other scripts, tables needing a different scale, or an established journal template.

Keep table headers with their content where feasible. Avoid a lone data row at the page foot, captions stranded from figures, clipped text, and unnecessarily large gaps. Use page-break and keep controls carefully instead of shrinking the whole document.

## Response letter

Preserve the reviewer's original comments, including references to the earlier manuscript. Update the author's response and the locations in the revised manuscript. Do not “correct” a reviewer's old figure number as if it were a current cross-reference.

The opening overview should normally fit one or two natural paragraphs. First/Second/Third can organize the main changes within those paragraphs. Summarize the reviewer concerns and the actual response across both writing and substantive research:

**Overview sequence:** acknowledge the revision context → group the reviewers' main concerns → summarize the corresponding conceptual, empirical, and discussion changes in a logical order → direct the reader to the point-by-point replies. A condensed overview must still preserve all major revision categories relevant to this resubmission.

- Introduction, research gaps, and literature synthesis.
- Conceptual foundations and the interpretation of the framework.
- Revised temporal or spatial scope and measurement, when actually changed.
- Added quantitative inequality, integrated access, validation, sensitivity, or scenario analyses, where actually performed.
- Theoretical and practical implications, figures, tables, and supplement.

Select the categories relevant to the revision. Do not mechanically mention every category or list every minor formatting operation. Make clear when new analyses strengthened the study rather than describing all revision work as improved writing.

For each comment, address the specific concern, identify the actual change or justified interpretation, and give a verified section, figure, table, or supplementary location. Similar comments may need related explanations, but do not paste irrelevant boilerplate or inflate a scenario into implemented policy.

**Individual-reply sequence:** identify the substantive concern → explain how the new writing or analysis addresses it → state the resulting clarification or new evidence → give the verified location. If a real new experiment addresses the concern, say so explicitly rather than describing it only as a rewritten paragraph.

Distinguish an intended change from a completed one. Never invent page numbers or claim a whole-manuscript rewrite unless the comparison supports it.

## Cover letter

Explain the submission or resubmission context, the manuscript's question and fit, and the major conceptual and empirical improvements. Keep the overview compact and ordered. In Brad's preferred revision cover letter, omit detailed results and percentages unless explicitly requested.

**Sequence:** submission/resubmission context → question and journal relevance → one or two compact paragraphs covering the main changes → courteous request for consideration. Align the revision categories with the response letter while keeping the cover letter at overview level; do not import its comment-by-comment detail or results catalog.

Ensure the title, journal, previous manuscript identifier, editor correspondence, and author details are accurate and current. Do not invent an invitation, promise of acceptance, or editor approval.

If an author requests a bold sentence explaining why a fully rewritten manuscript uses black rather than red text, bold that sentence only. Do not apply the emphasis to the whole surrounding paragraph without instruction.

## Final package check

Check the files actually being uploaded, not merely a version believed to be identical. A hash comparison can establish identity; a package-part comparison can distinguish a metadata-only change from content changes.

Inspect the relevant cross-file relationships:

- Title, author order, affiliation, corresponding-author marks, and CRediT roles.
- Abstract, main results, tables, figures, supplement, highlights, and letter result statements.
- Units, denominators, populations, observation years, census composition, thresholds, and scenario conditions.
- Current captions, in-text targets, subsection numbers, and response-letter locations.
- Reference labels and fields, bibliography output, comments, tracked revisions, red text, placeholders, and broken field messages.
- Paragraph-role typography, figure readability, table pagination, and caption placement.
- Anonymous review copies, including document properties when anonymization is required; identified title pages and editor-only correspondence may have different handling.

Verify current journal rules when asked about venue compliance. Do not treat another journal's word limit or a generic submission checklist as verified rules for this venue. Word-count totals also depend on whether captions, references, and tables are included; name the counting scope.

A file-consistency pass is not a complete scientific audit. Give a scoped readiness judgment and list actionable unresolved items without claiming guaranteed acceptance or zero research-integrity risk.

## Deliverables and cleanup

For text-only requests, return text only. For document edits, return the intended final documents, not unsolicited QA PDFs or screenshots. Keep editable figure sources when requested.

Follow the user's actual storage policy. In Brad's local environment, new artifacts and temporary scripts must not be physically stored on C; use a verified E location, while respecting an explicitly named existing project destination on another drive. Do not hard-code personal project paths into this public skill.

Do not accumulate automatic historical figure ZIPs. Retain the current figures and necessary editable sources by default. Existing versions, manuscript files, and PDFs may be removed only within explicit cleanup authorization, with exact targets and a recoverable strategy where practical. Permission to revise prose does not include cleanup or repository publication.

## Detail-level execution

For author roles, paragraph-role fonts, English curly punctuation, native Word fields, selective bolding, lone-row pagination, highlights, anonymous properties, and release judgments, use [Submission detail checks](submission-detail-checks.md). Check actual dependencies instead of applying a whole-document normalization or a generic submission checklist.

Preserve the distinction between an author instruction and document content. A pasted example, reviewer request, or source manuscript is evidence for the current task; it does not independently authorize global reference-library edits, new experiments, public upload, authorship changes, or deletion.
