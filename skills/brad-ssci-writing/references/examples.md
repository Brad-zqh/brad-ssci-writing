# Synthetic calibration examples

All examples below were written for this skill. They are illustrations of rhetorical choices, not excerpts from a manuscript, reported empirical findings, or verified literature claims. They contain no real citations or invented study results.

## 1. Repair the missing premise

**Weak**

Service availability is an important feature of local provision. Residents have different needs. Therefore, perceptions should be included in the index.

**Better**

Local provision determines which services are available, while residents' needs shape which of those services they can use. Evaluating provision alone can consequently miss differences between an available service and one that meets users' needs. A consumer perspective gives attention to that relationship.

**Why**: the revision explains why the perspectives belong together rather than relying on “therefore” to bridge an unsupported jump. A publication would still need appropriate support for any literature or empirical claim.

## 2. Keep a conceptual framework conceptual

**Operational wording in the wrong section**

Perceived comfort was extracted from review images, assigned a bounded score, and averaged using annual weights.

**Concept-level wording**

Comfort concerns how users experience the physical setting. The same space may suit some activities while obstructing others, making users' needs relevant to its evaluation. This perspective connects the conditions provided with the experience of using them.

**Why**: data extraction and aggregation belong in Methods. The conceptual paragraph explains meaning and relevance without pretending that a proxy directly captures every aspect of experience.

## 3. Compress adjacent repetition

**Repetitive**

Differences remain unclear when service quality, fees, and walking access are considered together. We consider service quality, fees, and walking access together to compare differences between social groups.

**Better**

Differences remain unclear when service quality, fees, and walking access are considered together. Integrating these conditions allows a more detailed comparison across social groups.

**Why**: the second sentence advances the response to the gap without repeating the same three conditions. Use “allows” only when the actual analysis supports this claim.

## 4. Explain why proximity is insufficient

Nearby facilities may offer suitable services at charges some residents cannot afford. Conversely, lower-cost options within walking reach may provide less suitable services. Considering these conditions together distinguishes the presence of local provision from residents' opportunities to use it.

**Why**: the contrast gives the joint analysis a reason. It does not claim that the conditions have been empirically measured unless the study actually does so.

## 5. Motivate temporal analysis without doubling the wording

**Repetitive**

We trace the long-term evolution of the index to capture its long-term dynamics over time.

**Better**

We trace the index's long-term spatial and temporal evolution.

**A rationale when the paragraph needs one**

Openings and closures can change local provision, while services and user preferences may also evolve. A single assessment cannot establish whether improvements persist or how differences between places develop.

**Why**: the rationale explains the information missing from a snapshot; the study-action sentence states what is traced once.

## 6. Connect data sources by purpose

**Mechanical**

We collected facility records from a city portal. Furthermore, we obtained population data from a census agency. Moreover, we collected road-network records from a mapping source.

**Better**

We collected facility records from the city portal. To compare provision across population groups, we obtained census boundaries and population attributes from the census agency. We then used road-network records from the mapping source to estimate walking access.

**Why**: the links explain the role of each source. These generic source labels are examples, not data-provenance statements for a real study.

## 7. State a contribution at its actual level

**Inflated**

This revolutionary framework completely transforms how access is understood.

**More precise**

The analysis distinguishes local provision from usable opportunities by examining service conditions and travel constraints for the same destinations.

**Why**: the reader can identify what changes in the analysis. Whether it is an original theoretical contribution depends on the literature and evidence, not on the adjective.

## 8. Keep practical responsibility focused

**Diffuse**

Planners, agencies, operators, schools, charities, residents, researchers, housing organizations, and businesses should cooperate to improve services.

**Focused**

Municipal planners can use the identified service gaps to prioritize areas for further assessment. Site visits and resident feedback would help determine whether new provision or improvements to existing facilities are the more appropriate response.

**Why**: one main actor owns a decision. In a real paper, tie the recommendation to an actual finding and check the actor's responsibilities.

## 9. Remove defensive framing without overstating evidence

**Defensive**

To avoid misunderstanding, we emphasize that we are not claiming that our indicator measures actual service use.

**Direct**

The indicator measures opportunities to reach a suitable service.

**When a limitation is needed**

Visit records would allow future research to examine how these opportunities relate to actual use.

**Why**: the definition and future validation remain clear. Removing the warning does not justify calling opportunities observed visits elsewhere.

## 10. Summarize a revision compactly

**Illustrative overview structure**

First, we strengthened the introduction and literature synthesis and clarified the conceptual framework. Second, we extended the empirical analysis to examine how service conditions and access differ across population groups. Third, we added planning scenarios and expanded the validation analyses. Finally, we reorganized the discussion and updated the figures, tables, and supporting material to align with the revised analyses.

**Why**: the paragraph covers writing and substantive research in an ordered overview. Use only the changes actually made. A real response still needs specific, verified locations in the point-by-point replies.

## Scenario calibration

The following cases test editorial decisions, not a required final wording. Apply the relevant guide and check the observable boundary. A unit-test pass on the prose helper cannot establish these judgments automatically.

## 11. A contrast fragment

**Request:** “Is this link coherent?” The text ends one sentence with a desirable definition and begins the next with “While unsuitable services can create social costs.”

**Expected decision:** recognize the incomplete subordinate clause and determine whether the intended relation is contrast. Use a complete contrast sentence or attach a main clause as appropriate. Do not add three more general sentences or globally replace every “while.”

**Acceptance:** the revision is grammatical, preserves the claim's strength, and expresses the intended relationship with the preceding sentence. Any empirical claim still requires its real support.

## 12. A one-sentence comparison after defined criteria

**Request:** “Shorten the final sentence; do not repeat the criteria.” The preceding sentence already states that quality, charges, and walking access are considered together.

**Possible local revision:** “We integrate these conditions to compare opportunities across population groups.”

**Acceptance:** the backward reference has a clear antecedent, the comparison remains explicit, and no theoretical breakthrough or new measure is invented. If applying conditions to the same destinations is not yet clear, preserve that essential relation.

## 13. A demand-side concept paragraph

**Request:** “Explain why the user perspective matters; this is a conceptual framework.” The draft defines available services and immediately describes extracting review counts.

**Expected decision:** replace the operational detour with the relevant conceptual bridge between what is offered and what people select, accept, or experience. Leave extraction and weighting in Methods. Do not equate all those user-related concepts or pretend that every dimension directly measures all of them.

**Acceptance:** the paragraph explains meaning, foundation, setting, and relationship. It does not end with an empty repetition of its definition merely to mimic other paragraphs.

## 14. Source attribution despite an informal grammar label

**Request:** “Use passive voice, like ‘we obtained from,’ instead of saying the agency provided it.” The requested example shows the intended emphasis.

**Possible wording:** “We obtained facility records from the city portal. For the population comparison, we collected census boundaries and demographic attributes from the census agency.”

**Acceptance:** the source and action remain accurate, the second sentence explains its role, and no unnecessary grammar lecture or global voice conversion is added. The generic agency names are examples, not real provenance.

## 15. Removing repeated citation displays

**Request:** “The same source appears too often in these sentences.” The passage contains one supported proposition repeated twice and a separate new claim.

**Expected decision:** merge the duplicated proposition and retain its managed citation anchor. Check whether the new claim actually has support. Do not delete all citation fields, replace them with typed names, or move one citation to imply support for both claims automatically.

**Acceptance:** attribution is clear, live reference management remains intact, and brevity is not achieved by silently weakening evidence.

## 16. One stakeholder per practical paragraph

**Request:** “Reduce the number of stakeholders; do not substantially change the content.” The paragraph already gives a finding-linked service-improvement recommendation but lists several agencies, institutions, and operators.

**Expected decision:** retain the recommendation and its evidentiary basis, assign one plausible main actor, and remove redundant actor lists. Do not invent a new subsidy, regulatory power, partnership, or additional experiment.

**Acceptance:** responsibility is clearer, the substantive advice is preserved, and the action's feasible scope is not overstated.

## 17. A short but complete revision overview

**Request:** “Cover all main revisions in one or two paragraphs, including the new analyses. No results in the cover letter.”

**Expected decision:** privately map the actual reviewer concerns and completed changes, then summarize them in ordered groups. Retain conceptual and writing work, expanded empirical work, validation, and practical analysis only where actually done. Keep details and verified locations in the individual replies.

**Acceptance:** substantive work is not reduced to “improved writing”; the overview does not become many paragraphs; the cover letter does not acquire a numerical results paragraph. The original comments and actual resubmission context remain accurate.

## 18. Curly punctuation and role-based fonts

**Request:** “Use this English apostrophe and make fonts consistent.” The desired word is “residents’,” while nearby title-page paragraphs or tables have mixed sizes.

**Expected decision:** inspect the apostrophe and its run font, preserve the selected English curly form, and normalize comparable paragraph roles. Do not convert it to ASCII merely because curved marks were initially described as “Chinese.” Do not force titles, body text, table notes, and native equations into one size or font.

**Acceptance:** the chosen visual form is retained and appropriate role distinctions survive. A rendered line break is not treated as a missing space on extraction alone.

## 19. A final check with an excluded issue

**Request:** “Ignore the earlier evidence discussion; just tell me if these upload files match the checked copies.”

**Expected decision:** compare the actual files. Distinguish unchanged content from metadata-only differences, and inspect affected cross-file relationships. Keep a positive consistency conclusion explicitly within that scope.

**Acceptance:** the response does not reopen the excluded debate, alter files, or claim all evidence and research-integrity questions are resolved. Renaming a file is not described as a manuscript revision.

## 20. Updating values without redesigning a figure

**Request:** “Use the corrected analysis values, but keep the original plotting code and visual design.” A heatmap exists and a similarly named Python file is nearby.

**Expected decision:** trace the exact plotted input and configuration before replacing values. Retain the existing code/style; repair only a necessary local label, range, or spacing issue and report it. Synchronize dependent text and letters only within authorization.

**Acceptance:** no matrix or configuration is guessed, the figure still matches its source, and a public code update does not silently add a repository link or availability claim to Word files.
