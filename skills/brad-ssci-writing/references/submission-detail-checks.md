# Submission detail checks

Select the checks relevant to the authorized task. This guide does not authorize research changes or certify readiness automatically. Use [revision scope](revision-submission.md) for authority and [evidence rules](evidence-integrity.md) for claim support.

## D01. Identify the actual upload set

Inspect the exact files identified by the author, including similar filenames and renames. Use hashes to establish byte identity. If they differ, compare content, fields, media, and styles before concluding the research changed.

A save timestamp, ZIP-container change, or author-property change is not necessarily a text revision. The same name or size is not proof of identity. Never replace a newer author edit with a folder copy merely because its version number looks final.

## D02. Separate allowed changes

Privately distinguish requested wording/format repairs, authorized dependent synchronization, and substantive decisions requiring communication. Source support, numerical conflicts, analysis changes, and authorship interpretation belong in the last category.

Excluding a known issue from a later check narrows that check; it does not resolve it. Respect the scope without repeatedly reopening excluded topics or falsely certifying the whole study.

## D03. Maintain the cross-file value map

Compare applicable abstract, text, captions, tables, SI, highlights, and letter statements for years, observation units/counts, denominators, thresholds, precision, uncertainty, reference groups, and scenario conditions.

Different percentages may have different denominators. Establishments and records are different units. Investigate before declaring inconsistency; do not average versions or choose a favorable result. Trace the current analysis to its source and discuss substantive changes before synchronizing them.

## D04. Align evidence and chronology

Check dataset, network, population-composition, attribute, and outcome years. Reusing population composition must not become a claim that a new census was measured. Missing historical evidence must not silently become zero or a fabricated observation.

Keep household and person weights distinct. Maintain distinctions between local conditions, potential reach, observed behavior, and simulated benefit throughout the files, not only in one limitation note.

## D05. Follow numbering dependencies

Compare caption inventories with in-text references, section references, and response-letter locations. Check panel letters, supplementary prefixes, equation numbers, ranges, and substantive correspondence, not merely whether a number exists.

If a framework moves into the literature review or caption material moves into the text, update affected author replies and locations when authorized. Preserve a reviewer's original references to the earlier submission. Do not renumber intentional supplementary ordering without checking every dependency.

## D06. Handle authorship only as instructed

When moving an author, preserve unaffected authors' relative order. Verify ordinal placement, including a penultimate position. Update affiliations, corresponding marks, emails, signatures, and metadata only as applicable to the authorized change.

Removing correspondence status does not remove authorship or change contributions. Moving an author does not establish funding acquisition. Record authorized CRediT roles consistently; do not infer grant ownership or rewrite acknowledgments from author order. Never turn one project's author arrangement into a public skill default.

## D07. Normalize by paragraph role

Distinguish title/subtitle, headings, body, captions, table headers/body/notes, references, author information, and declarations. Use consistent font and size within a role, with intentional exceptions.

Brad's ordinary Word defaults are Times New Roman English text, approximately 12 pt body, and readable 10.5 pt table text. Do not force all titles, notes, symbols, equations, and tables into one size. Preserve native mathematical typography and appropriate non-Latin fonts.

For SI, use flush-left prose without first-line indentation and paragraph spacing when requested. Do not erase centered equations, hanging bibliography indents, lists, or intentional table alignment while changing body paragraphs.

## D08. Inspect punctuation and its font

A curved apostrophe is not automatically Chinese punctuation; English Unicode marks can look wrong in a fallback font. Inspect the character and run font. Preserve the chosen English curly marks in the surrounding Latin typeface; do not globally convert to ASCII.

Check possessive spacing, paired quotes, citation punctuation, extra spaces, broken words, and line-break artifacts. A rendered signature followed by a new line is not necessarily a missing space in extracted text. Verify appearance before correcting an apparent extraction defect.

## D09. Preserve Word internals

Inspect live citation fields/item mappings separately from their cached display. Check bibliography output, field errors, unresolved bookmarks, visible duplication, and stale author labels. Preserve native equations, fields, and cross-references.

Raw field counts do not establish repeated citations: tables of contents can contain nested PAGEREF fields counted differently by different readers. Extraneous archive metadata is not automatically a citation-frequency problem. Diagnose the visible output and its origin before changing Zotero or CSL.

Do not anonymize by indiscriminately stripping custom XML, add-in fields, or citations. Inspect document-cleanup behavior before applying it. XML display substitution is not proof of a successful Zotero refresh.

## D10. Check pagination and emphasis locally

Inspect lone rows at page feet, split rows, repeated headers, detached captions, and clipped/unreadable figures. Prefer a local page break or keep setting before shrinking all fonts or margins. A long table can span pages if readable.

Bold only the requested sentence/span. An emphasized clean-text presentation explanation does not imply the following explanatory sentences should be bold. Keep unrelated letter formatting unchanged.

Plain text cannot prove layout quality. Inspect affected rendered pages after layout changes and report only the extent actually checked.

## D11. Keep the revision overview complete but short

Use the requested one or two natural paragraphs while retaining the main reviewer concerns and actual responses. First/Second/Third can organize a paragraph without producing many new sections.

Separate writing changes from actual empirical additions: years/scope, quantitative comparisons, integrated constraints, external checks, sensitivity work, and scenarios. Include only changes made. Complete coverage is not a list of every technical setting.

Compare actual previous/current files before describing their difference. Do not invent contrasts or disparage the earlier study. A new experiment addressing a comment should be identified as research, not merely a rewritten paragraph.

## D12. Maintain the cover-letter boundary

Keep question, journal fit, revision context, and major change categories. Omit detailed numerical findings by default. Do not import the Results section or point-by-point reply table.

Verify title, previous identifier, editor correspondence, date, and signature. An invitation supports only what it actually says, not an implied acceptance commitment. Handle editor-only identified correspondence separately from anonymous review files.

## D13. Check highlights and declarations

Use distinct supported highlight messages. Verify current venue limits, including spaces in character counts, if asked about compliance. Do not treat a remembered bullet count or character limit as universal. Keep claims and numbers aligned with the manuscript.

Verify authorized funding, conflicts, AI-use, and data/code availability. Public source records, redistribution permission, and availability of derived outputs differ. Do not remove acknowledgments simply to anonymize without checking the workflow.

GitHub publication and mentioning a repository in Word are separate choices. Public code does not authorize a new manuscript link or availability claim when the author wants Word files unchanged.

## D14. Check anonymous identity at the required scope

When anonymity is required, inspect body text, comments, revision authors, properties, headers/footers, filenames, and identifying embedded material. Title pages and editor-only correspondence may retain author information.

Do not scrub every file indiscriminately or certify anonymity solely because author names are absent from the body. An excluded anonymity check cannot be reported as completed.

## D15. Bound readiness and cleanup claims

Keep distinct: file identity, cross-reference consistency, inspected layout, claim support, numerical reproducibility, and verified venue rules. State what was established. A passed consistency check is not guaranteed validity or acceptance.

Keep unrequested QA exports and scripts out of the upload set. Respect storage policy. Cleanup requires separate authorization, exact targets, preservation of excluded sources, and a recoverable strategy where practical. Do not delete old Word/PDF files or research assets as a polishing side effect.

## D16. Recheck after the author's final changes

Identify changed files and inspect affected relationships. Do not rely solely on an earlier pass, reopen an unrelated debate, or falsely mark an unresolved issue fixed.

Report concrete actionable differences. If no new inconsistency is found within scope, say so without inventing more rewrites to demonstrate effort.
