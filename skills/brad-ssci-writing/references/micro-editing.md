# Micro-editing decisions

Use the relevant decisions for a concrete author complaint, alongside the [chapter logic](section-roles.md). Do not append this checklist to a text-only answer or run every mode for every sentence. Examples are synthetic illustrations, not manuscript excerpts or empirical findings.

## M01. The sentences do not connect

Identify the missing relation: definition → relevance, provision → options, options → use, contrast → unresolved comparison, or change → its consequence. Carry an established object into the next sentence and supply the necessary premise inside the revised passage.

If a paragraph jumps from service attributes to user experience, explain how selection or use makes those attributes relevant. Merely adding “Furthermore” does not repair that jump. Do not leave the connecting explanation only in a note to the author.

## M02. The passage is too short or vague

Add what is missing: a construct's meaning, the relevant relationship, why the setting changes the question, or what a prior approach cannot reveal. Do not pad it with synonyms, another general definition, or a data inventory.

“Users have different preferences” may still be insufficient. Explain what those differences change about the meaning or evaluation of provision.

## M03. Cut it by about half

Retain the claim, necessary bridge, and scope. Remove duplicated purpose clauses, repeated criteria, empty significance statements, and details outside the section's job. Aim for the requested reduction without changing the research or losing attribution.

If the target length cannot retain an important qualification, identify the tradeoff outside the manuscript. Do not offer a tighter but unsupported claim.

## M04. Too much therefore

Check both repeated cadence and whether an inference is warranted. Combine linked sentences, use an explicit subject, make a substantive contrast, or omit a conclusion already evident. Do not simply replace every “therefore” with “thus.”

First/Second/Third paragraphs need not have identical endings. Retain an informative implication, not a definition repeated as a conclusion.

## M05. Add nevertheless or however

Locate the real contrast in the preceding context. Use the requested transition when it expresses that contrast, not to create opposition between compatible facts. A connector does not explain why an individual-level score cannot answer an environment-level question.

Distinguish contrast, an additional dimension, and consequence. Do not strengthen a limited gap into complete absence to make the opposition sound stronger.

## M06. A dependent clause is suspended

“While unsuitable services can create social costs.” is incomplete without a main clause. Supply the intended main clause or use a complete sentence expressing the relationship. If the preceding sentence sets up a contrast, “By contrast, unsuitable services can create social costs” may be appropriate.

Do not globally replace “while”: it can correctly express contrast, simultaneity, or subordination. Check sentence completeness and subject–verb agreement after the repair.

## M07. The purpose repeats the action

“We examine long-term change to capture long-term dynamics over time” says the same thing twice. State what is traced once. Keep a purpose clause only if it adds a distinct, supported reason, such as examining persistence or differences between places.

Check “compare group differences to analyze social inequalities” against the preceding sentence for the same problem. Do not replace repetition with an inflated theoretical claim.

## M08. Criteria are listed twice

Explain the conditions once, then use a clear backward reference such as “these conditions” or “these options.” Retain the important relation if the conditions must hold for the same destinations.

Do not list quality, cost, and walking access in a gap sentence and repeat the list immediately in the study-action sentence. Do not introduce “all three criteria” before the conceptual argument naturally establishes the conditions.

## M09. The label is opaque

For “jointly qualifying” or “destination-area conditions,” unpack what qualifies, the relevant conditions, and whether the attribute belongs to a service, destination, or surrounding area. After first explanation, shorten without repeatedly redefining it.

Do not silently turn “services in areas with favorable conditions” into “high-quality services” when the quality measure is area-level. Nearby provision is not automatically a usable option.

## M10. The author wants descriptive wording

State the comparison, factors, and population without adding “reconceptualizes,” “transforms,” or a theoretical breakthrough. A request for a concise description is not permission to inflate its rhetorical strength.

Conversely, a theoretical-contribution paragraph needs the changed understanding, not only a method list. Decide from the actual section and request.

## M11. The gap suggests nothing exists

Distinguish sparse evidence, a setting-specific boundary, separate rather than integrated measures, and a missing perspective. Preserve known prior examples. Existing assessments rule out a universal absence claim, but not a narrower gap.

Specify the remaining relationship, unit, group, temporal comparison, or integration. Repeatedly saying “limited” without its consequence does not motivate a study.

## M12. A place label distracts

Use “previous studies,” a verified author citation, or the relevant approach when a city's name adds no analytical information. Do not turn the place into a special method name such as “the City-X assessment.”

Retain geography when comparing contexts, explaining transferability, or describing a sample. Do not remove locations from reference titles or empirical descriptions.

## M13. An index name intrudes into a gap

Use the substantive concept when the study-specific acronym has not been introduced or adds nothing. A conceptual gap concerns what the phenomenon includes; an index label can wrongly recast it as a calculation gap.

After defining a measure, use its acronym where it improves precision. Do not globally substitute loose synonyms or repeatedly expand the same acronym.

## M14. Supply and demand are named but not connected

Explain the applicable bridge: provision sets choices, selection determines use, expectations shape acceptability, or experience informs evaluations. Choose the relationship supported by the construct and evidence.

Keep choices, frequency, preferences, attitudes, acceptance, and perceptions distinct. A perception is not automatically a revealed preference; a mention is not a verified purchase. The available consumer dataset is not itself the theoretical justification.

## M15. Walking access becomes awareness or walkability

Keep a price-and-distance argument on those constraints unless awareness is independently relevant and supported. Adding awareness merely because a source mentions it can derail the paragraph.

Walking access concerns reaching destinations under the defined network or time conditions; walkability can describe the pedestrian environment. Do not substitute these to vary wording when that changes the construct. Check the author's actual definition first.

## M16. Temporal motivation is copied across sections

Keep the common rationale but change its job: Introduction explains what a snapshot leaves unknown; the review compares prior designs; theoretical implications explain what the repeated observations now add.

Changing provision, services, and users can motivate temporal analysis without identical sentences in every section. Do not imply individual opening, closure, or behavior histories were observed when only repeated area summaries are available.

## M17. The same citation follows every sentence

Keep a clear anchor for the supported proposition and combine redundant statements when useful. Remove duplicate clusters only if attribution stays unambiguous. A different or stronger claim may need a separate direct source.

With managed references, remove or move the intended citation through the citation workflow. Do not delete cached author text while leaving a live field. Reducing repetition does not mean removing the theoretical foundation.

## M18. The grammar label conflicts with the example

If the author says “passive” but supplies “we collected from,” follow the concrete wording and source-attribution intention rather than blindly applying the label. “We obtained records from the portal” is active but can be exactly the intended form. Ask only if the difference materially affects the deliverable.

Preserve the real collection action and source. Do not turn each source sentence into “The agency provided…” when the requested emphasis is what researchers obtained. Connect sources by analytical purpose.

## M19. A short phrase is author-approved

Preserve harmless accepted choices such as “Several findings emerged,” First/Second/Third organization, or a chosen concise purpose phrase. Do not reopen these choices in every round unless a concrete grammatical, factual, or scope issue arises.

“Stronger” does not authorize a harsher claim. “Comprehensive” does not automatically mean a longer cover letter. Interpret the request in its local rhetorical context.

## M20. Check lexical and grammatical precision

After editing, check collocation, subject–verb agreement, spelling, articles, parallel lists, and tense. “Complement” or “build on” may express an extension of prior research; “implement previous studies” usually means a different action. Confirm the intended relation.

Use a conceptual present for definitions where appropriate and follow the established tense for completed analyses. Distinguish completed work from proposed or simulated outcomes. Do not impose a global tense swap.

Keep technical names, capitalization, hyphenation, and English variety consistent. Define unfamiliar acronyms where needed. Do not change constructs merely to avoid a repeated term.

## M21. The small edit changes the argument

Compare who, what, where, when, the comparison, evidence type, and inference strength before and after. Check neighboring sentences and citation scope. A smoother standalone sentence may be a worse paragraph if it loses the necessary relationship.

Prefer a reordered clause, explicit subject, or one connecting sentence when sufficient. Do not rewrite an accepted paragraph or insert operational detail to demonstrate effort.

## M22. Deliver the requested form

For “return the sentence only,” return it without a lecture, alternatives, or file mutation. For “is it coherent,” give a direct judgment and, if useful, one local alternative. For “change the document,” inspect and edit the actual current file with only authorized dependent changes.

Explain substantive concerns outside the manuscript. Do not claim a file was changed when only a suggested sentence was returned.
